Naturalist Overview

A quintessential native woodland spring ephemeral whose glossy, mottled leaves resemble the flanks of a brook trout. Arising in early April before canopy closure, it stores solar reserves deep within underground corms before vanishing completely below soil by midsummer heat.

Observable Diagnostic Traits

Examine in situ
1
Mottled foliage pairs

Two basal elliptical leaves mottled with purplish-brown or bronze speckling indicate a mature flowering specimen. Single leaves belong to juvenile corms requiring up to seven years to bloom.

2
Six reflexed yellow tepals

Under bright midday ambient light, all six golden-yellow floral tepals dramatically curve backwards toward the flowering scape, exposing reproductive organs.

3
Six stamens with yellow or brown anthers

Gently inspect inside the nodding cup. Note whether the prominent anthers are chocolate brown or yellow—both are standard natural morphotypes of E. americanum.
